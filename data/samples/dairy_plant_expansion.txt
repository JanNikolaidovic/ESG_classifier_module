The Westvale Dairy Expansion Project will increase the processing capacity of the sponsor's existing milk processing plant from 300,000 to 550,000 litres per day. The expansion includes a new cheese production line, an additional cold storage building and an upgrade of the on-site wastewater treatment plant. The plant is located on the edge of a small town, within an industrial zone, and is surrounded by farmland.

The total investment is 32 million EUR, of which 40% will come from the sponsor's own funds and 60% from a seven-year bank loan. The company has been profitable in each of the last five years, with revenue of 120 million EUR and EBITDA of 14 million EUR last year. Milk will be sourced from around 400 local farms under long-term supply agreements.

The plant currently discharges treated wastewater into a nearby stream. In 2021, the regional environmental authority fined the company for exceeding the permitted limits for organic load in its effluent on several occasions. The upgraded treatment plant is designed to meet the new discharge standards, but its performance has not yet been tested at full capacity. Water consumption will increase by about 70%, and the plant relies on groundwater wells that are also used by neighbouring farms for irrigation.

The expansion will create 120 new permanent jobs. The plant operates in three shifts, and workers handle cleaning chemicals, including caustic soda and nitric acid, as part of routine equipment cleaning. Two lost-time injuries were recorded at the plant last year. The workforce is not unionised.

The number of milk tankers and delivery trucks entering the site will roughly double, mostly using a road that passes through a residential area. The refrigeration system uses ammonia, and the sponsor plans to replace part of it with a new system as part of the expansion.

The company is family-owned and its accounts are audited annually. It does not have a formal environmental management system but intends to work towards certification after the expansion is completed.
