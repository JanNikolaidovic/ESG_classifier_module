The Kalanga River Hydropower Project is a 240 MW run-of-river hydroelectric facility to be developed by Northbridge Energy Ltd in the Upper Kalanga Valley. The project will supply electricity to the national grid under a 25-year power purchase agreement with the state utility. Total capital expenditure is estimated at 410 million USD, financed through 30% sponsor equity and 70% senior debt from a consortium of commercial and development banks.

The project includes a 38-metre diversion weir, a 6.2 km headrace tunnel, an underground powerhouse and a 54 km transmission line connecting to the Ridgeway substation. Construction is expected to take 48 months and will employ approximately 1,800 workers at peak, most of them through subcontractors.

The reservoir and associated works will require the acquisition of around 1,150 hectares of land. Approximately 340 households living in four villages along the river will need to be physically relocated, and a further 600 households will lose part of their farmland. Some of the affected land is used by the Mbeti community, an indigenous group with customary rights to the forest areas in the valley. Consultations with village leaders have started, but community representatives have raised concerns about the adequacy of the compensation offered and the loss of access to sacred sites located near the river.

The diversion weir will reduce water flow along a 9 km stretch of the river during the dry season. The river supports several endemic fish species, and the downstream wetlands are an important habitat for migratory birds. Tree clearing for access roads and the transmission line corridor will affect around 220 hectares of secondary forest. Sediment released during tunnel excavation could temporarily affect water quality for downstream communities that rely on the river for drinking water and fishing.

Construction will involve the transport and storage of explosives, fuel and cement additives at the site. Spoil from the tunnel excavation, estimated at 900,000 cubic metres, will be deposited in designated disposal areas. Heavy truck traffic on the existing village roads is expected to increase dust and noise levels and raises road safety concerns for local residents, especially children walking to school.

A large part of the workforce will be migrant workers housed in temporary camps. The main civil works contractor has previously been cited by the labour inspectorate for excessive overtime and for delays in the payment of wages. Workers on the site will be exposed to risks associated with underground tunnelling, working at height and heavy machinery.

The region is seismically active and has experienced severe flooding in recent years. Climate projections indicate more variable rainfall, which could affect both the generation output and the structural safety of the weir during extreme flood events.

In 2022, Northbridge Energy's former chief executive was investigated by the national anti-corruption agency in connection with the award of an earlier government concession. The investigation was closed without charges, but the company has since revised its board composition and is in the process of adopting a new code of conduct and whistleblower policy. The company does not currently publish a sustainability report.

The sponsor intends to prepare an Environmental and Social Impact Assessment in line with international standards, together with a Resettlement Action Plan and a Biodiversity Management Plan. Once operational, the project is expected to avoid approximately 600,000 tonnes of CO2 emissions per year by displacing coal-fired generation.
